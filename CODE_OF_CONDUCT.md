# Code of conduct

This catalog is a place where member organizations share work they are proud of and ask
each other honest questions about it. The code is short because the expectation is
simple: treat one another's shared work, and one another, as colleagues.

It applies to every submission, review thread, pull request, discussion, issue and
email that passes through this repository or the site it publishes.

## How we engage

- **Ask before you assume.** An entry is one team's account of their own work. If
  something is unclear, ask the contact — in the open, where the next reader finds the
  answer — before drawing a conclusion in public.
- **Credit what you reuse.** Submitting a resource never transfers ownership. If you
  adopt, adapt or borrow from another organization's entry, say so, and say it in your
  own entry when you publish it.
- **Feedback names the problem, not the person.** In review and in discussion, describe
  what does not work and what would; do not characterise the people who built it.
- **Reviews are decisions with reasons.** A request for revisions says specifically what
  to change. A declined submission always comes with a rationale, and the submitter may
  appeal to the full governance committee.
- **Keep private things private.** No personal or protected health information in
  entries, screenshots, example data, review threads or discussions. If you see some,
  tell the maintainers rather than quoting it.
- **Disagree in the open, decide by the process.** Disputes about a review decision go
  to the governance committee. Disputes about these rules go there too.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, personal attacks, discriminatory language, publishing someone's private
information, and sustained disruption of review or discussion. Nor is using the
catalog's channels to sell to member organizations.

## Enforcement

Report a concern to the maintainers at the contact address on the site's footer, or
privately to any member of the governance committee. Reports are handled by people who
were not involved in the incident. Responses range from a private word to removal of the
content or the person from the repository, at the committee's discretion. Nobody who
reports a concern in good faith will be penalised for it.
